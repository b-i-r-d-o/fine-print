Fine Print: what it does

A phone app with an AI agent that files your personal agreements, notices what's coming due, asks you plain questions about your situation, and does the next small task for you. It's a Swiss Army knife for everyday contracts, not a rentals app.

From sketch to spec

What the scope said, and what we know now

Scope said

"Upload an agreement; the agent asks a plain question and helps you act."

Now we know

It spots that the lease ended 2 months ago, asks if you'll renew, explains Alberta's one-month notice rule, asks for the landlord's missing address, and hands you an editable PDF notice.

Scope said

"A few quick profile questions."

Now we know

Three tap questions: self or household, age range, learn or review. They're for show in this build, and their future jobs are written down.

Scope said

"Themes for mass appeal."

Now we know

Beige by default, then blue and black, then maroon and olive with a serif font, which stays for the demo.

The "what if" moments

First use

Empty home with an upload icon and "Upload your first agreement!" The demo starts blank on purpose.

Blurry photo

"This photo is unfocused. Please retake it so the text is clear."

Not an agreement

"The document you've uploaded looks like [short summary]. Would you like to continue to store this document?" No review, no rejection.

Missing details

The agent asks, for example about the landlord's address. If the answer is no, that line is left blank on the notice.

Coming-soon controls

Email connect, the Learn path, and mortgage Review are visible and marked. Tapping them explains they're coming soon.

What stays

Theme, profile answers, agreements and pending actions stay during the demo. Lasting storage is decided in the technical plan.

Product decisions

Joann's calls

  • The rental is the full showcase and the mortgage is the half. Two agreement types keep it a Swiss Army knife, not a rentals app.
  • The demo starts from a blank first use. Real first-time use matters more than demo data.
  • Sign-in is labelled as demo, email is coming soon, and there's no Play Store install. Depth over breadth, and honesty with judges.
  • Profile answers are for show for now. Their real roles are written down for later.
  • Follow Alberta rules. The notice has to be legally right.
  • Three themes, female-friendly, with neutral theme names.
  • Guidance, not legal advice, stated clearly in the app. Central to what Fine Print is.
  • Ask about missing details instead of guessing.
  • A non-agreement upload is just offered for storage. No review, no rejection.
Outside this build

Deferred, later, and never

Deferred Implied but not built now
  • Real Google or Facebook accounts. Infrastructure that doesn't prove the idea.
  • Email connection and a forwarding address. Needs email access and a mail service.
  • The Learn path. Needs its own lesson content.
  • Full mortgage review. The depth goes to the rental this time.
  • Preloaded demo agreements. The video shows blank first use.
Future features Possible later enhancements
  • Household accounts: one custodian managing agreements for several people, shareable.
  • Age range tunes which agreements to expect and how much to explain.
  • Learn path with topics matched to life stage, like RRSP agreements versus truck lease payments.
  • Pre-signing review: 5 highlights, relevance taps, "are you okay with this?", issuer summary.
  • Calendar integration for key dates, linked to the contract and clause.
  • Learning over time from answers and usage.
  • Folder linking and Dropbox, Google Drive or phone files storage.
  • Comparing a new contract version against the old one.
  • Every province and contract type.
Non-goals Things this project won't do
  • Native iPhone or Android apps, or app store installs.
  • Legal advice. It explains public rules and drafts a standard notice; it doesn't replace a lawyer.
  • Sending the notice for you. Delivery stays with the user.
  • Provinces other than Alberta for the tenancy rule, for now.
