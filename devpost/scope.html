Fine Print

A Swiss Army knife AI agent on your phone for every personal agreement. It files your contracts, notices what's coming up, asks you plain questions about your situation, and helps you take the next step.

The unique kernel

It reads, notices, asks, and then helps you act

Fine Print doesn't just store or summarize a contract. It notices what matters for your situation, asks you a plain question, and does the next small task with you.

How it fits together

The person, the core loop, and the proof

Who it's for

Everyday people with a life full of agreements

Leases, mortgages, phone and internet plans, credit cards, warranties. They sign, never reread, then scramble when something comes due, "scanning through 20 pages of small print looking for a date or dates, if that even exists."

Why it matters to Joann

A decade of contracts work, brought home

In professional contract management, no agreement is signed and forgotten. Fine Print brings that discipline to personal life.

"Helping me have an option to organize in my life, do minor actions and get these things off my mind."
What "working" looks like

The demo, step by step

Video staging, not an app feature

Phone home screen with medium-to-large icons. Page 1 has everyday apps: calendar, email, phone, text, and a silly game. Swipe to page 2, where Fine Print sits among related apps such as banking, files and wallet.

  1. Tap the Fine Print icon. The app opens.
  2. "Welcome! Please sign up or sign in." Google and Facebook buttons, labelled as demo.
  3. "Upload documents for storage now, or set up your user profile?" Profile.
  4. Profile: yourself or household, age range, learn or review. Review.
  5. "Connect your email (coming soon), or upload agreements for review." Upload a rental agreement.
  6. "This fixed-term tenancy ended 2 months ago. Are you looking to renew with your landlord?" "No, I want to move out." The "oh, that's cool" moment.
  7. The agent explains Alberta's rule, one full tenancy month's written notice, and offers to draft it. Yes.
  8. "I've drafted the notice for you, please review. Next step: deliver it to your landlord." Printed and mailed off-app.
  9. Home: the rental agreement is highlighted with "action pending."
  10. Upload a signed mortgage. "I've noted these dates and actions…" Store.
The boundary

What's in now, future features, and what's cut

Guiding rule · Joann's call

Depth over breadth. Effort goes into the demo flow that shows how usable Fine Print is. Features outside that flow can appear as non-functional, clearly marked "coming soon" or "demo," and be explained in the submission.

In now

The proof of concept

7
  • One app that runs on a phone, in the browser, saveable to the home screen with its own icon
  • Sign-in screen (demo, no real accounts) and profile questions
  • Agent chat driving the flow; three themes (beige default, blue and black, maroon and olive with a serif font)
  • Upload an agreement, with real AI reading of it
  • Rental agreement, the full showcase: spot the ended term, ask intent, explain the Alberta notice rule, draft the notice
  • Mortgage agreement, the "half": identify as signed, list key dates and actions, store
  • Home list of agreements with status and pending actions
Future features

Later, for "What's next"

9

Any of these can appear in the app as a "coming soon" control.

  • Connecting email to find agreements automatically, plus a personal forwarding address
  • Full pre-signing review: 5 highlights tied to your profile, relevance taps, "are you okay with this?", issuer summary
  • "Learn about contract topics" path
  • Real Google and Facebook sign-in and accounts
  • Learning over time from your answers and usage
  • Calendar integration for key dates, linked to the contract and clause
  • Real folder linking on desktop, plus Dropbox, Google Drive and phone files storage
  • Comparing a new contract version against the previous one
  • Deep built-in knowledge for every common contract type and province
Cut

Not in this build

2
  • Separate native iPhone and Android apps. Weeks of work, plus a Mac and developer accounts. One phone-ready app shows the same experience.
  • Installing from the Play Store in the demo. Needs a published native app. The demo starts at the home-screen icon instead.
