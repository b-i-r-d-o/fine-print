Fine Print

An AI agent on your phone that files your personal contracts and, when you're about to sign, turns the clauses that matter to you into plain yes/no questions.

The unique kernel

It knows a little about you, and asks "are you okay with this?"

Fine Print doesn't just summarize a contract. It connects clauses to what you told it about your life, then asks you about them in plain language. It gets more relevant as you tell it what matters.

The person, the core loop, and the proof

Who it's for

Everyday people with a life full of agreements

Leases, mortgages, phone and internet plans, credit cards, warranties. They sign and never reread. The sharpest moment is someone at the lawyer's office about to sign a mortgage, under pressure, with 20+ pages of small print.

Today they skim, trust, and hope, or end up "scanning through 20 pages of small print looking for a date or dates, if that even exists."

Why it matters to Joann

A decade of contracts work, brought home

In professional contract management, no agreement is signed and forgotten. Fine Print brings that discipline to personal life.

"Helping me have an option to organize in my life, do minor actions and get these things off my mind."
What "working" looks like

The demo, step by step

  1. Tap the Fine Print icon on a phone home screen. The app opens.
  2. Switch the theme from soothing professional to colourful personality.
  3. Home screen: "Welcome to your agreements!" Demo agreements fade down the list, named like 2026-10-02-xyzbank-mortgage.pdf. Agent box at the bottom: "Would you like to upload a new contract?"
  4. Type "yes". The upload (+) and camera icons get circled in yellow.
  5. Camera mode: framing cues, "next page" prompts, and a "done" button.
  6. Send. "This is a mortgage agreement with XYZ Bank. Filed as [name]. It isn't signed. Are you reviewing this for signing?"
  7. "No." "Stored as [name]-unsigned." Then "wait, I mean yes," and the review continues.
  8. "This is a very standard mortgage agreement…" Amortization, fixed rate (e.g. 4.2%), three more points, and an item of note specific to this mortgage. The agent connects a clause to a setup answer. You mark each highlight relevant or not. This is the "oh, that's cool" moment.
  9. "Learn more?" Yes. Three questions with answers, such as "How does this compare to other mortgages?": what's typical, and what this one adds.
  10. "Are you comfortable with these points? Review more?" No. A summary of points to raise with the issuer, and "upload the new version when they send one."
The boundary

What's in now, future features, and what's cut

In now

The proof of concept

8
  • One app that runs on a phone, in the browser, saveable to the home screen with its own icon
  • Short profile setup, plus a relevant / not relevant tap on highlights
  • Agent chat box driving the flow; theme switch (at least soothing and colourful)
  • Demo agreement list using the date-party-purpose.pdf naming convention
  • Contract intake by multi-page photo and by upload, with real AI reading of the contract
  • Identify type and issuer, name it, detect signed or unsigned, store it
  • Signing review: 5 highlights + item of note, 3 learn-more questions, comfort check, issuer summary
  • Mortgage as the showcase contract type
Future features

Later, for "What's next"

7
  • Learning over time from your relevance answers and usage; profile questions revisited at intervals
  • Calendar integration: suggested date highlights with links back to the contract and clause
  • A personal forwarding email address for sending contracts in
  • Real folder linking on desktop, plus Dropbox, Google Drive and phone files storage
  • Comparing a new contract version against the previous one
  • Deep built-in knowledge for every common contract type: rental, credit and bank card, mobile, internet, warranties and more
  • A third theme
Cut

Not in this build
